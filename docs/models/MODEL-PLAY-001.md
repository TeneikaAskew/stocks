## What it decides

Per ticker, a set of **entry cards**. Each card names a setup, a direction, a confirmation
checklist and a target / stop, and ends with the line

```
**IF ALL CONFIRMED -> {direction} ENTRY**
```

(`:305`, `:332`). Cards are generated by `build_all_cards` (`:391`), rendered as markdown by
`generate_card` (`:271`), and — when the job passes `--write-db`, which the scheduled
deployment does (`gcp/deploy.sh:4430`) — upserted as structured records into `playbook_cards`
by `write_playbook_cards` (`:942-993`). `/api/playbook` serves that table to the admin-facing
playbook UI.

**`playbook_cards` and `playbook_cards_staging` are different tables with different writers.**
This job writes the first. The user-style backtest endpoint in
`platform/api/routers/backtest.py:875` writes only the **staging** table, and the comment at
`:610-611` records that the UI reads `playbook_cards` and never the staging copy. A reader who
finds the `INSERT INTO playbook_cards_staging` in the router and concludes the API owns these
cards has the wrong writer.

## How a card's numbers are produced, and why the status is Experimental

`compute_card_stats` (`:70`) scores each card's pattern with `_score_trades` (`:114`): every
occurrence of the pattern is treated as a trade entered at the signal bar's close and exited by
the first of target, stop, or a time stop, with target and stop detected intrabar from
High / Low and the stop assumed first when both fall in the same bar. That exit model is
careful — pessimistic on same-bar ambiguity, clipped to the signal bar's own session so an
overnight gap cannot count, and explicit that occurrences with no forward bar are excluded
from the denominator rather than coerced to zero (CLAUDE.md §3.7, cited in its own docstring).

Two properties of the surrounding method are not careful, and together they are why this is
**Experimental** rather than a promotion-grade status:

- **The reported statistics are in-sample.** There is no train / test split anywhere in the
  module. `_score_trades` scores every occurrence of the pattern over the same frame the card
  was defined on, so `win_rate` and `avg_return_bps` describe the data the card was fitted to.
  A card's win rate is therefore not an estimate of its forward win rate, and the UI presents
  it without that distinction.
- **`best_horizon` is an argmax over four holds scored on that same data.** The card is scored
  at each of `HOLD_HORIZONS = (5, 15, 30, 60)` minutes (`:67`) and
  `best_horizon = max(valid, key=avg_return_bps)` (`:110`). Selecting the best of four on the
  evaluation set inflates the selected number by construction, and the selected hold is the one
  the card surfaces.

- **The statistics do not score the rule the card tells you to trade.** A card displays a
  setup, then a confirmation checklist, then `IF ALL CONFIRMED -> ENTRY`. What reaches
  `compute_card_stats` is the **setup mask alone**; the checklist items are strings passed to
  `generate_card` for display. Card 1 scores every occurrence of

  ```python
  mask = (prev2 == '2U') & (prev == '2U') & (labels == '2U')          # :434
  ```

  while telling the reader to enter only when RSI is 40-65, price is above VWAP, price is
  above the fast EMA, the 30-minute ORB trend is bullish and the EMAs have crossed. None of
  those five appears in the mask, so the published win rate describes a **strictly larger**
  population than the trades the card instructs. Checked across all twelve call sites by
  reading the mask actually passed:

  | Cards | Mask contains its own confirmations? |
  |---|---|
  | 1-11 (continuations, reversals, outside bar, ORB breakout and failure, support, resistance, order block) | **no** — label patterns, and for 6-8 an ORB-state term |
  | **12 — FTFC Maximum Conviction** | **yes**: `(ema_cross == 1) & (orb_trend == 1) & (labels == '2U') & rsi.between(40, 65)` (`:703`) |

  Card 12 is the one card whose number answers its own instruction, and it shows the others
  could. Confirmations only narrow, so for cards 1-11 the reported win rate is not a
  conservative estimate of the confirmed rule — it is a number about a different rule.

- **No costs are modelled.** `compute_card_stats`'s own docstring states it: *"All returns are
  price-only basis points — NO costs (commission / slippage / spread) are modelled anywhere."*
  On a 5-minute hold at a target measured in tens of basis points, that omission is the same
  order as the edge being claimed.

## Rationale

**Partly recorded.** The exit model is derived and its predecessor is named: the docstring
records that `win_rate` replaced *"the old 'did the next 1-minute bar tick up' proxy, which
ignored the card's own `target_bps` / `stop_bps` entirely"*, and that the forward window is now
clipped to the session because *"the old next-bar proxy silently let it through"*. The comment
at `:36-41` records a second corrected defect — per-ticker display strings scored against
hard-coded IWM basis points.

**UNKNOWN — not recorded:** the per-ticker `target_bps` / `stop_bps` in `TICKER_PROFILES` (`:46`), the
30-minute default time stop, and the four hold horizons. None carries a derivation, and the
selection among them is what the card publishes.

## Tests

`tests/scripts/test_scripts.py` covers the CLI surface. There is no test asserting that a
card's published win rate holds out of sample, which is the gap the status records.

## Known issues

**None filed.** Four method findings, established by reading the module and re-checked
against every `compute_card_stats` call site on 2026-09-21: in-sample statistics, an argmax
over four holds on that same data, no costs modelled, and — the one a user is most likely to
act on — eleven of twelve cards scoring a setup the card's own checklist then narrows. None
has been triaged into an issue; they are stated here rather than left to be rediscovered.
