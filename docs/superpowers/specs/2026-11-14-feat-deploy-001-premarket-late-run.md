## Problem

The owner trades SPY, QQQ and IWM from the morning brief. The brief's premarket high and low stop at
about 08:20 ET, because the premarket refresh runs at 08:20 and the brief at 08:30. In the owner's
words: "I actually want to see if we can find some way to update [premarket] to actually be 9:20. Or
maybe even 9:25 a.m. ... would I still get it around 9:30 a.m.? And then the insights brief, how long
would that take?"

### What the early snapshot misses (given)

Measured on 469 sessions per ticker, 2025-01 to 2026-11, read-only (scratch analysis
`/tmp/claude-0/-home-user/fa186a7b-7434-50ad-97db-844fe591fc0f/scratchpad/pmgap/`, executions
`db-query-dm96g` and `db-query-5gf2r`; not re-run for this spec):

| Measure | Share of sessions |
|---|---|
| The premarket high or low moves after 08:20 | about 55% |
| The 09:30 open is already outside the range known at 08:19 | 27 to 29% |
| The 09:30 open is outside the full premarket range | 2 to 3% |
| The final premarket extreme is set at or after 09:20 | about 17% |
| The final premarket extreme is set after 09:25 | 12 to 14% |

One session from this draft's own reads (live, `db-query-jxfn5`): on 2026-11-13 the 08:30 brief
recorded SPY premarket high 759.737 (snapshot fetched 08:20:57 ET); the nightly job later recorded the
full premarket high as 762.693; the signal monitor logged `put_reanchor: SPY open=759.9950` at
09:30:27 ET. The session opened above the high the brief was built on.

### Why the range matters beyond the number shown (read)

The premarket high and low are inputs to the levels, not decoration. `lib/strat_levels.py:2215-2216`
sets `cleared_above = max(reference, pre_high)` and `cleared_below = min(reference, pre_low)`, and
`_pick_side` (`:2226-2227`) lists every candidate between the reference and that mark as broken and
never picks it as a trigger. A level that premarket trades through after 08:20 therefore stays a live
trigger in the 08:30 brief, in the canonical `premarket_analysis` row, and in what the signal monitor
loads at the open.

### What the refresh computes, and from which window (read, live)

- The window is 04:00 to before 09:30 ET of whatever bars the vendor returns at fetch time. The
  bounds are `PREMARKET_START = time(4, 0)` and `REGULAR_SESSION_OPEN = time(9, 30)`
  (`lib/indicators.py:519-520`, applied at `:609`). There is no 08:20 or 08:30 in the computation:
  the end of the window is the fetch time. A run at 09:20 reads through about 09:19 with no change to
  the fetcher or to `calculate_premarket_context`.
- The fetch asks for real-time bars: `'entitlement': 'realtime'` and `'extended_hours': 'true'`
  (`gcp/fetchers/fetch_market_data.py:108-109`), one call per ticker for the whole month, kept to the
  requested date (`:156-157`).
- The only 08:30 that is code and not a comment is the replay clock,
  `_strat_observation_clock` (`gcp/premarket_brief.py:900-908`), which stamps a `BRIEF_AS_OF` replay's
  weekly, monthly and FTFC observation at 08:30 ET. `gcp/premarket_brief.py:858` matches an 08:30
  economic release for the ORB window and is unrelated.
- The brief does not compute premarket. It reads the session's own `market_data_daily` row
  (`premarket_snapshot_from_daily`, `gcp/premarket_brief.py:1354`, `lib/strat_levels.py:2418`).
  `compute_premarket_levels` (`lib/strat_levels.py:626`) only turns `pre_high` and `pre_low` into the
  PMK_H and PMK_L levels.
- Vendor freshness at 08:20 (live, execution `fetch-premarket-refresh-c47zg`, 2026-11-13): the job
  fetched between 08:20:35 and 08:20:58 ET. QQQ, NVDA, AMD, AVGO and MU each returned 261 bars, which
  is every minute from 04:00 through 08:20, so for those the newest bar was the minute in progress.
  SPY returned 253 bars where `market_data_intraday` now holds 261 for the same minutes
  (`db-query-jxfn5`), and its recorded high 759.737 equals the 08:15 bar's high while the stored bars
  show 759.75 at 08:04 and 08:06. So the real-time reply was not identical to the bars stored later.
  The job logs bar counts, not timestamps: which SPY minutes were absent at fetch time is not checked.

## Approaches considered

1. Move the early run to 09:20 instead of adding one. Closed by the owner's decision. It costs
   nothing extra and loses the brief for the hour before the open, which the owner uses.
2. Add the late run at 09:25 instead of 09:20. Closed by the owner's decision. It would recover the
   sessions whose final extreme is set between 09:20 and 09:25, about 3 to 5 points of the 17% (given).
   Against that, the refresh takes 36 to 63 s from trigger to its write and the brief 44 to 79 s (live,
   nine executions each), so a 09:25 run lands at about 09:27:30 and leaves about two minutes before
   the signal monitor's read at 09:30, and none if the refresh retries once. The measurements show a
   thin margin, not that it cannot land.
3. Schedule only, no code: a second refresh at 09:20 and a second brief at 09:22 with
   `BRIEF_UPDATE=true` in the scheduler body. Refused. It relies on a fixed two-minute gap, so a slow
   or failed refresh makes the brief rewrite the canonical row from the 08:20 snapshot while presenting
   it as late; it records a scheduled run as `manual_update`; it either posts the whole brief a second
   time or posts nothing; and nothing stops a write after the monitor has read.
4. One new job that runs the refresh and then the brief in one execution. Refused. The brief would
   know the refresh finished, but it needs a new job with a new entry module, and it either refreshes
   50 tickers before a three-ticker brief or duplicates the refresh for three.
5. Chosen: two scheduler entries at 09:20, both existing jobs, and the brief in a late slot that waits
   on the data. The late brief does not assume a gap: it starts with the refresh and holds until every
   brief ticker's `market_data_daily.pre_fetched_at` is at or after 09:20:00 ET of the session, with a
   deadline. The refresh stays the only writer of the snapshot and the brief stays a reader.

## Design

### The late run, end to end

All times America/New_York. Timings marked "live" are from the nine most recent executions of each
job (refresh: 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-13, all scheduled; brief: 2026-11-06 to 2026-11-13, six scheduled
weekday runs, the Sunday run, one manual and one replay); the chain as a whole has not run and its
landing time is an estimate.

| Time | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 09:20:00 | Cloud Scheduler fires `premarket-refresh-late` (job `fetch-premarket-refresh`) and `premarket-brief-late` (job `premarket-brief`, with `BRIEF_RUN_SLOT=late`). | this spec |
| 09:20:36 to 09:21:03 | The refresh upserts today's premarket columns for its universe of up to 50 tickers, with `pre_fetched_at` after 09:20. | live: trigger to completion 36 to 63 s |
| 09:20:03 to 09:20:53 | The brief's container starts. In the late slot it first checks that today is a session, then waits for the refresh's write. | live: creation to start 2 to 53 s; prerequisite |
| about 09:21:30 to 09:22:30 | The late brief builds the levels from the 09:20 snapshot, rewrites the canonical live row, appends history and posts to Discord in the form the owner picks (decision 2). | estimate: live run time 44 to 79 s; prerequisite |
| 09:27:00 | Deadline. If the refresh's write has not landed, the late brief writes nothing, says so in Discord and exits nonzero. The 08:30 levels stand. | prerequisite |
| 09:29:30 | Cutoff. The late brief never writes the canonical row at or after this time. | prerequisite |
| 09:30:00 to 09:30:30 | The signal monitor's first poll loads the levels then in the canonical row. | read, live |

So the owner's question "would I still get it around 9:30" is answered: the late brief is expected at
about 09:22, eight minutes before the open.

### How the brief knows the refresh finished

Not by the clock. The refresh stamps every row it writes with `pre_fetched_at`, the instant it
fetched that ticker's bars (`gcp/fetchers/fetch_premarket_refresh.py:204`; the column is live,
`db-query-jxfn5`). The late brief reads that column for its own tickers and proceeds only when every
one is at or after the slot's start, 09:20:00 America/New_York on the session date, built with the
named zone and compared as instants (CLAUDE.md 3.9). A refresh that is slow, retried or failed
therefore cannot be mistaken for one that finished: the early row's stamp is 08:20 and does not pass.
The wait is one keyed query every 5 s and ends at the deadline.

This is a statement about the fetch time, not about how fresh the vendor's newest bar was (decision 6).

schedulers` or `all` would create a 09:20 entry that runs the brief without the late slot: a second

makes that order mechanical: this PR's CI fails until `RUN_SLOTS` is on main.

### Why this is two specs

The schedule is `gcp/deploy.sh`, which the gate assigns to FEAT-DEPLOY-001. The behaviour that makes a
second run useful (overwrite the canonical row, wait on the refresh, stop before the open, post a short
note, skip a holiday) is `gcp/premarket_brief.py`, FEAT-PLAYBOOK-001. The same split, in the same
order, was used for the watched-ticker ingest
(`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-14-feat-deploy-001-watched-tickers-deploy.md`). It would become three
or four specs only if the owner picks a late insight run (decision 1, FEAT-INSIGHT-001) or a signal
monitor that re-reads (decision 3, FEAT-SIGNAL-001).

## Capacity

Both late entries run existing jobs a second time on each weekday. Rates for compute are the ones
`docs/audits/COST_AUDIT_2026-09-06.md:227` uses (0.000018 USD per vCPU-second, 0.000002 USD per
GiB-second); they were not re-read from the price page for this spec.

Late refresh (`fetch-premarket-refresh`, 1 vCPU, 512 MiB, task timeout 300 s, live):

- Volume: up to 50 tickers; one vendor reply per ticker holding the month's 1-minute bars to date;
  up to 50 rows of about a dozen columns upserted. Reply size in bytes: not checked.
- Velocity: 50 vendor calls; about 105 SQL round-trips (3 universe reads, 1 parameter read, 1 column
  check, 50 prior-session reads, 50 upserts in one transaction).
- Wall-clock: 26 to 54 s start to completion over nine executions (live). The task timeout is 5.5
  times the slowest.

Late brief (`premarket-brief`, 1 vCPU, 1 GiB, task timeout 1800 s, live):

- Volume: the brief tickers (3 today); 3 canonical rows rewritten, 3 history rows appended.
- Velocity: the early brief's reads and writes; plus the wait, at most 84 keyed queries (one per 5 s
  for 7 minutes); plus up to 8 model calls if decision 4 is yes.
- Wall-clock: 44 to 79 s start to completion over nine executions (live); the late slot adds the
  wait, expected under 65 s and bounded at 7 minutes by the deadline. The task timeout is 3.6 times
  the worst case (499 s).

## Risks

- The wiring merges or deploys before the late slot exists in the image. Caught by the fourth
  `done_when` item in CI, and by the order in Close-out.
- A late entry is created with the wrong cron, zone or body. Caught by the helper's read back, by the
  recording-stub test, and by the `gcloud scheduler jobs describe` step in Close-out.
- The late brief fails every day and nobody pauses it (CLAUDE.md rule 0.6). It exits nonzero into the
  existing failure lane; the remedy is to pause `premarket-brief-late`, which Close-out names. The
  early run is a separate entry and is not affected.
- An existing entry changes by accident. The first `done_when` item requires an additions-only diff,
  and the test pins the seven neighbouring entries.
- The playbook outcome statistics change meaning. From the first late run, the resolver at 21:15
  grades the 09:20 levels, not the 08:30 ones; `premarket_analysis_history` keeps both. The first
  session is recorded in Close-out so the series break has a date.
- On Mondays the canonical row changes more than on other days (see "Found while reading").
- The model registry, the inventory and the catalog count drift from the fleet. Caught by the fifth
  and sixth `done_when` items; the count is a close-out step because it describes the live fleet.

## Found while reading (not changed by this spec)

- Every Monday's canonical row is written on Sunday evening. The brief's date is `date.today()` in a
  container whose clock is UTC (`gcp/premarket_brief.py:893`; live: the job sets no `TZ`), so the
  Sunday 21:00 ET run computes Monday's date and writes Monday's live row, and Monday's 08:30 run then
  finds a live row and skips the canonical write. Live, `db-query-p2k6q`: for SPY, 22 of 22 Mondays
  since 2026-06-01 have a canonical row built the day before, with no premarket snapshot. The site and
  the signal monitor therefore use a Sunday-evening basis on Mondays. With the late slot, Monday's
  canonical row would be rewritten at about 09:22, a larger change than on other days; the 08:30 to
  09:22 window on Mondays stays as it is unless this is fixed on its own. Read but not run: the same
  date also makes the Sunday run's `is_sunday` test false (`gcp/premarket_brief.py:1296`), so the
  week-ahead earnings view it was scheduled for is not selected.
- A `BRIEF_AS_OF` replay does not see what the live brief saw. It reads the session's
  `market_data_daily` row, which the nightly fetch has overwritten with the full 04:00 to 09:30
  premarket (`gcp/fetchers/fetch_market_data.py:456-457`, `:519-520`). Live, `db-query-jxfn5`,
  2026-11-13: the live 08:30 brief recorded SPY 759.737 and QQQ 691.94 as premarket highs; the rows a
  replay would read now hold 762.693 and 693.13. The late run narrows this gap (09:19 against 09:29)
  and does not close it.
- The task brief for this draft described the replay path as one that rebuilds premarket for a past
  date. As read, it does not rebuild: it reads the stored row, as above.
